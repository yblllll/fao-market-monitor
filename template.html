Rebuild of an FAO GIEWS product · August 2026

Agricultural Market Monitor

A working replication of FAO's Food Price Monitoring and Analysis tool, rebuilt from the live GIEWS API: international commodity quotations, the SDG 2.c.1 food price anomaly screen across 76 countries, and a regression on what actually moves grain prices.

Independent work, not an FAO publication. Built by Yibin Li from FAO's public data API. FAO publishes the official version of these indicators through the FPMA Tool; anomaly values here are computed independently and may differ from FAO's published figures (see Method).

Layer 1 · International quotations

What world markets are quoting

Every international price series FAO tracks, with the change over one month, one year and three years, and the SDG 2.c.1 anomaly score for the latest month. Sort any column; filter by commodity group.

Layer 2 · Origin spreads

The same grain, priced by origin

Export quotations for one commodity across every origin FAO monitors. The spread between origins is where trade policy, freight and harvest timing show up, and it is what an import-dependent buyer actually faces.

Layer 3 · Domestic prices, 76 countries

Where staple prices are abnormal

National-average retail prices, deflated by each country's own CPI, then screened with SDG 2.c.1. The indicator asks whether this month's price growth is unusual for this month, in this market, so it separates a genuine anomaly from ordinary seasonality.
Layer 4 · Trade network

Who ships the calories, and to whom

Bilateral trade in eight staples, from FAO's detailed trade matrix. Click any country to pin it: the arrows then show its largest export destinations and import origins, with width scaled to tonnage. Colour on the map is whichever measure you pick, including the price-anomaly screen from the layer above.

Layer 5 · Price drivers

What moves grain prices, and when

Monthly log-difference regressions of export quotations on Brent crude and Black Sea urea, zero to six lags, Newey–West standard errors. Run on the full sample and again from 2019, which brackets the fertiliser price shock.

Method

SDG 2.c.1, Indicator of Food Price Anomalies. Following FAO's published metadata: a compound growth rate is computed over three months and over twelve months, CGR = (P_t / P_{t-k})^(1/k) − 1. Each is standardised against the distribution of that same calendar month across all available years, so seasonality is differenced out rather than assumed away. The two standardised components are combined as IFPA = 0.4·Quarterly + 0.6·Annual. A score below 0.5 is normal, 0.5 to 1.0 is moderately high, and 1.0 or above is abnormally high.

One departure, stated plainly. FAO's metadata specifies a weighted mean and standard deviation across years but does not publish the weighting scheme. This rebuild uses equal weights and requires at least five historical observations of the same calendar month. Scores here are therefore indicative and will not match FAO's published values exactly. Domestic series are screened on CPI-deflated prices, international series on nominal US dollars.

Pass-through regression. Δln(grain) = α + ΣβⱼΔln(Brent)ₜ₋ⱼ + ΣγⱼΔln(urea)ₜ₋ⱼ + ε for j = 0…6, estimated by OLS with Newey–West HAC covariance at six lags. The cumulative elasticity is the sum of a block's coefficients and its standard error comes from the full HAC covariance matrix, so the covariance between lag coefficients is carried rather than dropped.

Data. All series retrieved from the FAO GIEWS FPMA Tool v4 public API. Underlying sources include the International Grains Council, USDA, Oil World, the FAO rice price update, APK-Inform, the International Sugar Organization, Dairy Market News and the World Bank Pink Sheet; domestic series come from national statistical offices and market information systems. Series with fewer than 36 monthly observations are dropped.
