Rebuild of an FAO GIEWS product · August 2026

Agricultural Market Monitor

A working replication of FAO's Food Price Monitoring and Analysis tool, rebuilt from the live GIEWS API: international commodity quotations, the SDG 2.c.1 food price anomaly screen across 76 countries, and a regression on what actually moves grain prices.

Method

SDG 2.c.1, Indicator of Food Price Anomalies. Following FAO's published metadata: a compound growth rate is computed over three months and over twelve months, CGR = (P_t / P_{t-k})^(1/k) − 1. Each is standardised against the distribution of that same calendar month across all available years, so seasonality is differenced out rather than assumed away. The two standardised components are combined as IFPA = 0.4·Quarterly + 0.6·Annual. A score below 0.5 is normal, 0.5 to 1.0 is moderately high, and 1.0 or above is abnormally high.

One departure, stated plainly. FAO's metadata specifies a weighted mean and standard deviation across years but does not publish the weighting scheme. This rebuild uses equal weights and requires at least five historical observations of the same calendar month. Scores here are therefore indicative and will not match FAO's published values exactly. Domestic series are screened on CPI-deflated prices, international series on nominal US dollars.

A data-quality flag. Where a national CPI is rebased, the implied deflator jumps and the real price change becomes an artefact rather than a measurement. Any series whose deflator moves more than 15 percent in a single month inside the comparison window is marked with a dashed outline and an exclamation mark. Four countries are affected in the current window: Djibouti, Iraq, Nigeria and Tajikistan. Tajikistan is the clearest case, where the deflator jumps 242 percent in January 2025 and the real wheat-flour change reads +30 percent against a nominal change of −4 percent.

Pass-through regression. Δln(grain) = α + ΣβⱼΔln(Brent)ₜ₋ⱼ + ΣγⱼΔln(urea)ₜ₋ⱼ + ε for j = 0…6, estimated by OLS with Newey–West HAC covariance at six lags. The cumulative elasticity is the sum of a block's coefficients and its standard error comes from the full HAC covariance matrix, so the covariance between lag coefficients is carried rather than dropped.
